> **Authority note:** This standard **operationalises** Mission Team loading and staffing-capacity principles implied by APPROVED ADR-0015 and required by the Mission Operating System. This document remains **DRAFT** (GAP-032 not closed). This standard does **not** invent universal numeric staff ratios, maximum class sizes, duty hours or teacher-registration law. Those remain **country / implementation** (`GAP-037`). Detailed Mentor caseload principles: **`PEO-005`** (`GAP-034` proposed — not closed).

---

## 1. Purpose

`PEO-001` answers who may be authorised.  
`PEO-002` answers whether authority remains current.  
`PEO-003` answers how adults are deliberately developed.

This standard answers:

> How does a real Tarbiyat school determine whether it has **enough appropriately authorised, current and developed adults** to operate its Mission system safely and educationally well?

This is **not** simply a student∶teacher ratio.

Central principle:

**Mission capacity must follow available competent human capacity — not the other way around.**

A school must not create more Missions than its people can safely and meaningfully support.

This document proposes resolution of **GAP-032**. It is **not** a Mentor caseload standard (`GAP-034` — see `PEO-005`), country registration / statutory ratio map (`GAP-037`), or authorisation / currency / development syllabus (`PEO-001`–`003`).

---

## 2. Core principle — no universal staff ratio

Do **not** define one universal staff ratio.

Tarbiyat staffing capacity depends on factors such as:

- learner stage;
- Mission authenticity and educationalisation quality;
- Mission risk (`MIS-003`);
- World / domain;
- supervision need;
- tools / facilities;
- learner independence;
- practitioner authorisation (`PEO-001`);
- practitioner currency (`PEO-002`);
- Mentor demand (detail → `PEO-005` / GAP-034);
- partner / I-in-R support;
- assessment / evidence workload;
- safeguarding needs;
- Formal Recognition / exam obligations.

A safe low-risk Creative Mission may need a different adult pattern from an R2 engineering fabrication Mission.

---

## 3. Capacity ≠ headcount

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Headcount** | Number of adults present or employed |
| **Capacity** | What those adults are **competent, authorised, current and available** to do at the relevant time |

**10 adults ≠ 10 interchangeable staff.**

A school may have many Educators but insufficient:

- technical supervision;
- Mentor capacity;
- Mission Leads;
- safeguarding coverage;
- elevated-risk practitioners.

Only adults who meet the **function + authorisation + currency + availability** test count toward that function’s capacity.

---

## 4. Mission Team functions

Evaluate Mission capacity across ADR-0015 functions (and supporting roles), for example:

- Educator;
- Practitioner;
- Mentor;
- Mission Lead;
- Technical / Lab Professional;
- safeguarding / governance coverage;
- Specialist Professional Services (as needed);
- external / I-in-R practitioners.

**Do not require one separate person for every function.** One competent adult may perform multiple **compatible** functions when authorised and current.

**Prevent impossible role concentration** — e.g. one person cannot simultaneously provide direct elevated-risk technical supervision in two rooms, full Mentor caseloads, and sole safeguarding escalation for the whole school without proportional redesign.

---

## 5. Mission complexity dimensions

Use practical dimensions — **not** a prestige ranking or giant scoring algorithm:

| Dimension | Examples of what changes load |
|---|---|
| A. Learner stage | Discover facilitation intensity vs Produce independence |
| B. Number of learners | Group size and subdivision |
| C. Duration | Sustained vs short cycle |
| D. Number of Worlds | Cross-World competency breadth |
| E. Risk level | R0–R4 (`MIS-003`) |
| F. Technical complexity | Tools, processes, specialist judgment |
| G. Partner involvement | Coordination and Education-First pressure |
| H. External / community contact | Safeguarding and logistics |
| I. Deployment level | Public / live / irreversible outputs |
| J. Data sensitivity | Privacy and access controls |
| K. IP / Creator Rights complexity | Triggers and adult conflict boundaries |
| L. Travel / fieldwork | Off-campus supervision |
| M. Evidence / assessment burden | Observation, documentation, Formal Recognition overlap |

Complexity informs required functions and peak support — it does not invent a Core points system.

---

## 6. Risk-driven staffing (MIS-003)

| Risk posture | Staffing implication |
|---|---|
| **R0 / R1** | Often supports larger groups / lighter specialist coverage when educationally sound |
| **R2** | Stronger competent supervision required |
| **R3** | Restricted participation + specialist controls; capacity scarce by design |
| **R4** | **Not** made permissible by adding more staff |

**More adults cannot convert prohibited work into acceptable work.**

---

## 7. Authorisation-driven staffing (PEO-001)

Only appropriately **authorised** people count toward a required function.

Example: three adults present, but only one authorised for laser-cutter supervision → technical capacity for that activity is **one**, not three.

Unauthorised presence is not Mission capacity.

---

## 8. Currency-driven staffing (PEO-002)

Expired, restricted, refresh-required, supervised-return or suspended permissions must **not** be counted as full available capacity for the affected scopes.

Current staffing capacity uses **current permission state** (`MIS-FRM-007` + latest `MIS-FRM-008` as relevant).

---

## 9. Development capacity (PEO-003)

Schools need capacity not only to deliver Missions but to **develop future capacity**.

Protect time for induction, shadowing, co-facilitation, coaching, peer observation, development and currency refresh.

Do **not** run the school at 100% utilisation such that no development is possible. No Core utilisation percentage is mandated.

---

## 10. Mission Lead loading

Mission Lead load depends on **Mission complexity and concurrency**, not simply Mission count.

One person might coordinate several small R0/R1 Missions, but not safely or effectively many complex multi-partner R2/R3 Missions at once.

Avoid universal numeric caps in Core. Implementation may later establish local planning ranges.

---

## 11. Practitioner loading

Depends on domain, learner independence, risk, tool use, Mission phase, supervision intensity and number of simultaneous groups.

Avoid both extremes:

- assuming one practitioner = one Mission always;
- assuming one practitioner can supervise unlimited Missions.

Direct supervision cannot be double-counted across simultaneous locations (§18).

---

## 12. Educator loading

Educator workload includes conceptual instruction, Mission preparation, facilitation, feedback, assessment/evidence, collaboration, Formal Recognition / exam requirements and documentation.

**Do not count only contact hours.**

---

## 13. Technical / Lab loading

Technical capacity must consider facility opening, equipment preparation, safety checks, learner induction, supervision, maintenance, consumables and incident response.

**A lab is not available merely because the room exists.**

---

## 14. Mentor capacity — detailed in PEO-005

Mission Team capacity must recognise that **Mentor availability is a real constraint**.

No school should claim strong human pathway guidance if Mentors carry impossible caseloads.

**Detailed Mentor caseload / human-guidance capacity:** `PEO-005` (`GAP-034` proposed resolution — not closed). This standard requires that Mission / school capacity checks acknowledge Mentor demand where pathway guidance is claimed.

---

## 15. Safeguarding capacity

Safeguarding must be available at an appropriate level.

Avoid requiring every Mission to have one dedicated safeguarding employee.

Instead define:

- functional coverage appropriate to activity;
- escalation availability;
- appropriate supervision.

Higher-risk / external-contact activity may require stronger presence. Country safeguarding law remains country detail.

---

## 16. Partner / I-in-R capacity

External partners and I-in-R may add useful capacity.

They do **not** replace:

- school accountability;
- core safeguarding;
- required educational judgment (`MIS-001` / `MIS-002`).

Do not build a staffing model dependent entirely on volunteers or corporate goodwill.

---

## 17. Staff absence and resilience

A real model must handle sickness, leave, practitioner withdrawal, I-in-R named-person change, currency lapse and training absence.

**Resilience principle:** a Mission should not become unsafe because one uniquely authorised adult is absent without a planned response.

Possible proportional responses:

- substitute authorised / current adult;
- reduce scope or risk;
- pause elevated work;
- use simulation / lower-authenticity redesign;
- reschedule;
- change activity.

---

## 18. Single-point-of-failure

Identify critical functions where one-person dependency creates operational risk, for example:

- only R3-authorised technical practitioner;
- only Mission Lead covering too many concurrent complex Missions;
- only safeguarding escalation person;
- only critical data / IP administrator for sensitive Missions.

Do **not** require universal duplication. Capacity planning should **identify** critical dependencies and plan contingencies.

---

## 19. Concurrent Missions

Capacity must account for Missions happening at the **same time**.

A practitioner cannot be counted simultaneously in two locations for direct supervision.

Avoid simple annual totals that hide timetable conflicts.

---

## 20. Peak load

Mission demand changes by phase:

| Phase (illustrative) | Typical pressure |
|---|---|
| Research / framing | Often lighter technical supervision; stronger Educator / Lead framing |
| Build / test | Intensive Practitioner / Lab support |
| Deployment / public output | Safeguarding, data, technical review, Creator Rights triggers |

Plan for **peak phases**, not only average load.

---

## 21. Cross-World Missions

Cross-World Missions may need multiple competencies.

Do not assume a single adult covers all Worlds. Do not force one specialist from every tagged World when substantive work does not require it.

World significance thresholds remain later design (`GAP-047`).

---

## 22. Developmental stage

| Stage (illustrative) | Capacity tendency |
|---|---|
| Discover | More facilitation / supervision may be needed despite lower technical depth |
| Choose | Greater learner autonomy begins; pathway / Mentor demand matters |
| Deepen | More specialist Practitioner / Lab support |
| Produce | Potentially greater technical / deployment complexity with stronger learner independence |

No universal age ratios in Core.

---

## 23. Staffing capacity states (not school rankings)

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ADEQUATE** | Required functions available with authorised, current, concurrent capacity and contingency |
| **ADEQUATE WITH CONDITIONS** | Operable if stated conditions hold (reduced group, extra supervision, limited tools, named contingency) |
| **CAPACITY CONSTRAINED** | Redesign, delay, stagger, reduce authenticity/risk, or add capacity before launch |
| **NOT SAFE / NOT OPERABLE** | Do not launch / pause elevated work — insufficient competent capacity or prohibited risk |

These guide Mission go / no-go / redesign. They are **not** prestige ranks for schools.

---

## 24. Capacity check before Mission launch

Before launching a Mission, confirm (proportional to complexity/risk):

- required functions;
- named authorised people for those functions;
- current permissions for required scopes;
- risk supervision posture (`MIS-003`);
- Mission Lead;
- technical / lab support where needed;
- Mentor availability where pathway guidance is claimed;
- safeguarding escalation coverage;
- timetable / concurrency conflicts;
- peak-phase coverage;
- contingency for absence / currency change.

If insufficient: **redesign, delay, reduce, simulate, or do not launch.**

Record: `PEO-FRM-002` (§28).

---

## 25. Capacity change during Mission

Re-check capacity when:

- a critical practitioner leaves or is absent;
- risk increases;
- scope expands;
- partner / named I-in-R person changes;
- learner numbers increase materially;
- deployment begins;
- tool / facility changes;
- authorisation or currency state changes.

Launch approval is **not** permanent.

---

## 26. No utilisation maximisation

Reject industrial logic such as “every practitioner must be 100% allocated.”

Healthy capacity needs slack for incidents, mentoring, reflection, development, coordination, unexpected learner needs and innovation.

Do **not** set a Core utilisation percentage.

---

## 27. Formal Recognition / exam load

Because Tarbiyat retains formal recognised assessment (ADR-0003):

capacity planning must include exam preparation, required curriculum coverage, documentation and external assessment obligations where they overlap Mission periods.

Do not design board-specific workloads here (country / implementation; MY mapping `GAP-002`).

---

## 28. Staff wellbeing (principle)

Unsustainable adult loading damages learner safety and Mission quality.

Protect preparation time, recovery, development, reasonable workload and professional judgment.

Do not turn this into detailed HR law.

---

## 29. Capacity record — `PEO-FRM-002`

**`PEO-FRM-002` — Mission Team Capacity Check**  
`docs/08-people-and-governance/templates/PEO-FRM-002-mission-team-capacity-check.md`

Typical fields: Mission identity; stage; learner group; phases; risk; required functions; named authorised/current adults; technical/lab coverage; Mentor requirement; safeguarding coverage; external contribution; concurrency conflicts; peak-load risks; contingencies; capacity state; launch decision; re-review triggers.

No numeric Core ratio required.

---

## 30. School-level portfolio capacity view

`PEO-004` also defines a **conceptual** school-level portfolio view (not a software dashboard or database schema).

Track, lightly:

- active Missions and concurrency;
- Mission Lead loading;
- authorised / current practitioner pool by domain/risk;
- lab / technical capacity;
- Mentor availability signal (detail → `PEO-005` / GAP-034);
- high-risk work;
- peak periods;
- bottlenecks;
- single points of failure.

This later informs scheduling / software design without inventing UI as Core law.

---

## 31. Resource-poor school principle

Do **not** design Tarbiyat so only wealthy schools can operate it.

A smaller or resource-constrained school may:

- run fewer concurrent Missions;
- use lower-risk workflows;
- use shared specialists;
- use digital / remote practitioners where safe and authorised;
- stagger Missions;
- use community expertise under `MIS-001` / `PEO-001`;
- use external facilities;
- emphasise lower-resource authentic work.

It must **not fake capacity it does not have**.

**Quality > quantity.**

## 32. Scenario tests

| ID | Scenario | Classification | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | One Educator + one Practitioner; 12 Discover learners; low-risk local mapping | **ADEQUATE** (or **WITH CONDITIONS** if Mentor claim overstated) | Low risk; proportional team; stage-appropriate |
| **B** | One laser-cutter-authorised practitioner; 30 learners want simultaneous fabrication | **CAPACITY CONSTRAINED** / redesign | Technical capacity = one authorised person; stagger groups or reduce simultaneous tool use |
| **C** | Four Missions share the same Mission Lead | **ADEQUATE WITH CONDITIONS** if all light R0/R1 and staggered; else **CAPACITY CONSTRAINED** | Lead load follows complexity + concurrency, not count alone |
| **D** | I-in-R engineer normally supervises; absent two weeks | **CAPACITY CONSTRAINED** → substitute / reduce / pause elevated work | Resilience; partner capacity non-substitutive for school accountability |
| **E** | Cross-World water Mission needs Engineering + Earth | **ADEQUATE WITH CONDITIONS** if both competencies covered (people or co-facilitation); else **CONSTRAINED** | Cross-World ≠ one adult by default |
| **F** | Mentor supports 150 learners while also teaching full time | **CAPACITY CONSTRAINED** for pathway claims; see `PEO-005` / GAP-034 | Mentor availability is a real constraint; caseload detail in PEO-005 |
| **G** | PA-3 practitioner becomes REFRESH REQUIRED mid-Mission | Re-check → often **CONSTRAINED** / pause affected scopes | Currency-driven capacity; launch not permanent |
| **H** | Produce learners highly independent; preparing public deployment | **ADEQUATE WITH CONDITIONS** | Independence helps build/test; deployment still needs review / safeguarding / IP triggers |
| **I** | Excellent labs; no authorised technical staff | **NOT SAFE / NOT OPERABLE** for elevated tool work | Room ≠ capacity |
| **J** | Practitioner “supervises” two rooms at once for direct elevated work | **NOT SAFE / NOT OPERABLE** (or redesign to one room / lower risk) | Concurrency; no double-count of direct supervision |
| **K** | Low risk but partner demands unrealistic deadline | **CAPACITY CONSTRAINED** / Education-First redesign | Partner pressure ≠ automatic overload; `MIS-001` |
| **L** | Exam period overlaps Mission build phase | **ADEQUATE WITH CONDITIONS** or **CONSTRAINED** | Formal Recognition load must be planned; stagger or reduce Mission intensity |
| **M** | Only safeguarding escalation lead absent | **ADEQUATE WITH CONDITIONS** if named deputy; else **CONSTRAINED / pause** elevated external-contact work | Single-point-of-failure |
| **N** | Small resource-poor school; limited specialists | **ADEQUATE** if fewer/lower-risk/staggered Missions; **NOT OPERABLE** if it fakes high-risk concurrent portfolio | Quality > quantity |
| **O** | Mission workload leaves no time for development/reflection | **CAPACITY CONSTRAINED** | Development slack required (`PEO-003`); reject 100% utilisation |

## 33. Core vs country vs implementation

| Layer | Includes |
|---|---|
| **Core** | Capacity follows competence/authorisation/currency; risk-sensitive loading; function-based staffing; concurrency; resilience; peak-load awareness; no unsafe launch; development slack; proportionality; capacity states; scenarios; resource-poor principle |
| **Country** | Statutory ratios; maximum class sizes; teacher registration; labour law; duty hours (`GAP-037`) |
| **Implementation** | Actual staff numbers; local planning ranges; schedules; FTE; calendars; allocation software; salary budgets |

## 37. Explicit non-claims

This standard does **not**:

- approve ADR-0015 or close GAP-032;
- invent a universal student∶teacher or Mission∶staff Core ratio;
- replace Mentor caseload architecture (`PEO-005` / GAP-034);
- invent country class-size or duty-hour law (`GAP-037`);
- allow more staff to legitimise R4 / prohibited work;
- treat capacity states as school prestige rankings;
- design allocation software or database schemas;
- require one person per faculty function on every Mission;
- endorse 100% adult utilisation.
