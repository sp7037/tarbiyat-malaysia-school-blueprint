> **Authority note:** This standard **operationalises** Mentor caseload and human-guidance capacity principles implied by APPROVED ADR-0015 and required wherever Tarbiyat claims meaningful longitudinal mentoring. This document remains **DRAFT** (GAP-034 not closed). This standard does **not** invent a universal Mentor∶learner Core ratio, counselling licence law, or statutory pastoral ratios. Those remain **country / implementation** (`GAP-037`).

---

## 1. Purpose

`PEO-004` answers whether a school has enough authorised, current and available adults to operate its Mission system.

This standard answers:

> How many learners can one Mentor genuinely know, guide and support before the role becomes **nominal** rather than **meaningful**?

Central principle:

**HUMAN GUIDANCE CAPACITY must reflect ACTUAL RELATIONSHIP DEPTH + AVAILABLE MENTOR TIME — not nominal assignment counts.**

Assigned learners ≠ meaningfully mentored learners.

This document proposes resolution of **GAP-034**. It is **not** a Mission Team launch-capacity standard (`PEO-004`), practitioner authorisation / currency / development syllabus (`PEO-001`–`003`), or country pastoral / registration map (`GAP-037`).

---

## 2. Core principle — relational, not administrative

Mentor assignment must remain **relational**.

A Mentor should reasonably be able to:

- know the learner as a person;
- understand developmental stage;
- review reflection and evidence at a proportionate depth;
- discuss interests and provisional concentration;
- notice disengagement or concern;
- support family dialogue where relevant;
- coordinate with Educators / Practitioners;
- help interpret options **without** making pathway decisions for the learner.

If assignment volume makes this impossible, the school does **not** have real mentoring capacity — regardless of roster labels.

---

## 3. Mentor ≠ everything

Clarify Mentor boundaries.

The Mentor is **not** automatically:

- counsellor;
- psychologist;
- therapist;
- safeguarding lead;
- subject teacher;
- Mission Lead;
- pathway decision-maker;
- PoC certifier;
- disciplinary authority.

The Mentor may **coordinate or refer**. Do not overload the role into universal adult responsibility.

Specialist Professional Services remain distinct (ADR-0015).

---

## 4. Caseload ≠ a simple number

Do **not** define one universal Core student∶Mentor ratio.

Mentor capacity may depend on dimensions such as:

| ID | Dimension | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| A | Learner stage | Discover / Choose intensity differs from stable Deepen patterns |
| B | Age / development | Younger learners often need more continuity |
| C | Transition intensity | Peaks tighten available caseload |
| D | Frequency / depth of mentoring | Weekly deep dialogue ≠ occasional check-in |
| E | Family-contact expectations | Dialogue load is real work |
| F | Learner support need | Temporary high-intensity cases reduce spare capacity |
| G | Safeguarding complexity | Escalation coordination consumes time; investigation is not Mentor work |
| H | Pathway uncertainty | Doubt and reversibility require dialogue |
| I | Number of active Missions (learner + Mentor) | Coordination and Mission load compete with mentoring time |
| J | Mentor’s teaching load | Full teaching load shrinks mentoring capacity |
| K | Mentor’s Mission responsibilities | Mission Lead / Practitioner duties are not free |
| L | Documentation / reflection burden | Continuity notes must stay light |
| M | Availability of specialist support | Referral pathways free Mentor capacity; their absence loads it |

Use these as practical judgment dimensions — **not** a giant scoring algorithm.

---

## 5. Developmental stage relationship

Stage informs intensity; it does **not** invent age ratios.

| Stage (illustrative) | Guidance tendency |
|---|---|
| **Explore** | Greater adult support and continuity may be needed |
| **Discover** | Reflection and Six Worlds exposure interpretation need meaningful Mentor time |
| **Discover → Choose** | Often among the **highest** guidance-intensity periods (see §16) |
| **Choose** | Ongoing review of provisional concentration and breadth |
| **Deepen** | More domain / pathway complexity; still needs relational continuity |
| **Produce** | Greater independence is common — but post-school / deployment decisions may **increase** need |

Do **not** assume older = less Mentor need in every situation.

---

## 6. Transition intensity

Identify periods where caseload capacity should **tighten** (no fixed Core multiplier):

- entering Discover;
- Discover → Choose;
- changing provisional concentration;
- major pathway doubt;
- major Mission failure or difficult Mission experience;
- safeguarding / wellbeing concern;
- Produce transition;
- post-school transition.

During such periods, schools should reduce new assignments, rebalance caseloads, or protect Mentor time rather than pretend average capacity still holds.

---

## 7. Contact model

Principle-level mentoring contact may include:

- regular individual conversation;
- small-group mentoring;
- ad hoc check-in;
- family dialogue;
- evidence / reflection review;
- transition conference;
- referral coordination.

Not every interaction must be 1∶1.

**Group mentoring must not replace individual knowledge of the learner.** Learners must retain access to private conversation, referral / escalation, and personal review at transition points.

---

## 8. Minimum relational standard — “meaningfully known”

At principle level, a Mentor should reasonably know:

- learner’s current stage;
- active interests / explorations;
- provisional concentration where applicable;
- major strengths / challenges;
- relevant Mission participation;
- significant concerns;
- current development goals;
- important family / context factors **where appropriately known**;
- next review needs.

Avoid invasive data expectations. “Meaningfully known” is a relational competence standard — not a surveillance dossier.

---

## 9. Documentation burden

Mentoring must not become paperwork-heavy.

Records should support continuity and accountability without turning every conversation into a form.

Proportionate artefacts may include:

- brief Mentor Note;
- transition summary;
- referral / escalation note;
- pathway review note.

Avoid daily logging. Heavy documentation that displaces relationship time is a capacity failure, not a quality win.

---

## 10. Mentor other duties — link to PEO-004

A person teaching full-time, leading Missions and mentoring learners does **not** have unlimited Mentor capacity.

Caseload must reflect concurrent load:

- teaching load;
- Mission Lead load;
- practitioner duties;
- pastoral / safeguarding roles;
- administrative duties;
- development / currency time (`PEO-003` / `PEO-002`).

**Other-duty loading reduces Mentor capacity.** Connect capacity judgments to `PEO-004` Mission Team loading principles: the same adult cannot be double-counted as fully available everywhere at once.

---

## 11. Group mentoring

Allow efficient shared mentoring where appropriate, for example:

- reflection circles;
- pathway workshops;
- portfolio review groups.

Preserve:

- individual visibility;
- private conversation access;
- referral / escalation;
- personal review at transition points.

Group efficiency must not erase personal mentorship.

---

## 12. High-need learners

Some learners may temporarily require greater Mentor attention, for example:

- repeated disengagement;
- pathway uncertainty;
- major transition;
- family conflict;
- wellbeing concern;
- difficult Mission experience.

Do **not** create diagnostic categories in Core.

Need should trigger:

- temporary intensity;
- specialist referral where appropriate;
- reduced Mentor capacity elsewhere if necessary.

High-need intensity is a **capacity event**, not a stigma label.

---

## 13. Safeguarding boundary

Mentor may notice and escalate concerns.

Mentor must **not**:

- investigate serious safeguarding cases alone;
- provide professional therapy unless qualified and authorised;
- keep unsafe secrets;
- bypass school safeguarding process.

Connect to existing safeguarding governance (`MIS-003` principles; country safeguarding law remains country detail). Mentoring quality includes knowing limits.

---

## 14. Family engagement

Define proportionate family engagement — especially relevant for:

- younger learners;
- pathway transitions;
- significant concern;
- major concentration decisions.

Learner voice must remain central.

**Family influence ≠ automatic pathway control.** Country / cultural implementation may vary (`GAP-037` / local practice). Mentors help dialogue; they do not surrender learner agency to parental preference by default.

---

## 15. Discover → Choose relationship

ADR-0018 (UNDER REVIEW) requires human-governed pathway preparation.

Mentor capacity must be sufficient to support:

- reflection on Six Worlds exposure;
- interest vs capability distinction;
- aspirations;
- evidence;
- uncertainty;
- reversibility;
- breadth preservation.

A software recommendation **cannot** substitute for Mentor dialogue.

If a school claims Discover→Choose guidance while Mentors lack time for that dialogue, the claim is not credible.

---

## 16. PoC / evidence relationship

Mentor may help learners:

- reflect on evidence;
- organise developmental narrative;
- understand capability claims.

Mentor must **not** automatically:

- create evidence;
- certify all PoC claims;
- inflate claims;
- decide pathway from PoC alone.

Cross-reference ADR-0016 (UNDER REVIEW) and later PoC operating gaps. Mentoring interprets; verification / certification remain governed elsewhere.

---

## 17. AI / digital tools

AI / software may assist with:

- scheduling;
- reminders;
- summarising learner-owned records (with appropriate consent / privacy);
- identifying missed check-ins;
- surfacing overdue reviews.

AI must **not**:

- replace Mentor relationship;
- classify learner identity;
- assign pathway;
- make safeguarding judgments;
- determine Mentor capacity automatically.

Dashboards inform humans; they do not outrank Mentor judgment (§31 Scenario K).

---

## 18. Coverage / absence

If a Mentor is absent, continuity principles apply:

- temporary cover by an appropriately prepared adult;
- access to essential continuity records (not full invasive files by default);
- urgent safeguarding escalation path remains live;
- transition handover for Discover→Choose / major pathway moments;
- learner continuity of relationship where feasible.

Avoid one Mentor becoming a single point of failure for an entire cohort’s guidance claim.

---

## 19. Caseload states (not performance rankings)

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **SUSTAINABLE** | Mentor can maintain meaningful knowledge and contact given other duties and current intensity mix |
| **SUSTAINABLE WITH CONDITIONS** | Operable if stated conditions hold (protected time, reduced new assignments, group + individual mix, temporary cover) |
| **OVERLOADED** | Rebalance required — reduce caseload, reduce other duties, or stop claiming full mentoring intensity |
| **UNSAFE / NOT CREDIBLE** | Mentoring claim is nominal; school must not present this as meaningful human guidance |

These guide assignment and rebalancing. They are **not** staff performance grades or school prestige ranks.

---

## 20. Caseload review triggers

Trigger review when:

- learner count rises materially;
- Mentor takes additional teaching / Mission role;
- transition season begins;
- high-need learners increase;
- Mentor absence / leave;
- staff turnover;
- safeguarding demands rise;
- school schedule changes;
- Discover→Choose cohort peaks;
- prolonged missed-contact patterns appear.

Do **not** rely only on annual review.

---

## 21. Resource-poor school model

Do **not** make human mentoring exclusive to wealthy schools.

Possible strategies:

- smaller mentor groups with clear primary Mentors;
- shared Mentor responsibilities with **one** primary Mentor ownership;
- staggered reviews;
- scheduled transition weeks;
- group reflection + individual check-ins;
- trained community Mentors where authorised (`PEO-001` principles);
- digital scheduling support.

**Do not fake mentoring** where one person nominally “mentors” hundreds without meaningful contact.

Quality of relationship > quantity of assignments.

---

## 22. Primary Mentor principle

Each learner in a regular Tarbiyat pathway should have **one clearly identified primary Mentor** for continuity.

Allow:

- secondary specialist support;
- temporary substitutes;
- multi-adult support teams.

Avoid multiple adults with no clear ownership. Primary Mentor ≠ sole adult in the learner’s life.

---

## 23. Specialist referral

Mentor quality includes knowing when to refer to:

- safeguarding;
- wellbeing professionals;
- specialist Educator;
- Practitioner;
- pathway / career expertise;
- learning support;
- family support.

Referral is strength, not failure. Mentors who never refer may be over-reaching; Mentors who always defer without relationship may be under-mentoring.

---

## 24. Mentor competence — link to PEO-001 / PEO-003

Mentor function should require:

- appropriate authorisation / preparation for the Mentor function;
- Tarbiyat development (stage / pathway / Six Worlds familiarity);
- safeguarding orientation;
- reflective communication competence.

Do **not** invent counselling licence requirements in Core unless country law requires them (`GAP-037`).

A relationally gifted adult who does not understand Six Worlds / Discover→Choose architecture is **not** fully competent for Tarbiyat pathway mentoring (Scenario O).

---

## 25. Mentor currency — link to PEO-002

Currency for Mentor function may include evidence of:

- recent mentoring practice;
- pathway architecture familiarity;
- safeguarding refresh;
- current school procedures.

No universal fixed Core expiry period is set here. Implementation / country may define review intervals. Currency for Mentor is about **remaining able to mentor well**, not annual form theatre.

---

## 26. Capacity record — `PEO-FRM-003`

**`PEO-FRM-003` — Mentor Caseload Review**  
`docs/08-people-and-governance/templates/PEO-FRM-003-mentor-caseload-review.md`

Typical fields: Mentor identity; other functions / load; assigned learners (counts by stage — not sensitive dossiers); transition cases; high-intensity cases; planned individual contact; group mentoring; family-contact load; referrals / escalations; absence / coverage; capacity state; action / rebalancing; review triggers.

Do **not** require individual sensitive learner details in the portfolio-level record beyond what is necessary for capacity judgment. No database schema is mandated.

---

## 27. School-level Mentor capacity view

Define conceptually (not as software Core law):

- Mentor count;
- active Mentor capacity (after other-duty loading);
- learner assignment distribution;
- transition peaks;
- overloaded Mentors;
- uncovered learners (no primary Mentor or prolonged missed contact);
- absence risk / single points of failure.

This later informs scheduling / dashboards without inventing UI as Core law. Connect to `PEO-004` school-level portfolio view: Mentor signal there is detailed here.

---

## 28. Numeric-ratio decision

**Core does not set a universal fixed Mentor∶learner ratio.**

Country / implementation may establish planning ranges **after pilot evidence** (§29). Do not invent numbers to make the standard look concrete.

If a jurisdiction mandates pastoral ratios (`GAP-037`), those apply as country constraints — they still do not prove Tarbiyat “meaningful mentoring” by themselves.

---

## 29. Pilot / evidence

Because Mentor intensity is a human-capacity question, planning ranges should later be informed by:

- pilot operation;
- time studies;
- learner feedback;
- Mentor feedback;
- transition outcomes;
- safeguarding quality;
- missed-contact rates.

Do **not** close architecture by guessing ratios. This DRAFT proposes principles and states; numeric ranges remain open for evidence-informed implementation.

## 30. Scenario tests

| ID | Scenario | Classification | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Mentor has 20 Discover learners and no teaching duties | **SUSTAINABLE** (or **WITH CONDITIONS** if Discover→Choose peak or high-need cluster) | Focused mentoring load; still watch transition intensity |
| **B** | Mentor has 80 learners plus full teaching load | **OVERLOADED** / often **UNSAFE / NOT CREDIBLE** for meaningful guidance claims | Assignment volume + other duties make relational standard unlikely |
| **C** | Mentor has 35 learners, but 12 are in Discover → Choose | **SUSTAINABLE WITH CONDITIONS** or **OVERLOADED** | Transition intensity tightens capacity; protect time or rebalance |
| **D** | Mentor runs three Missions and mentors 40 learners | **OVERLOADED** (unless Missions are light and mentoring is lightly claimed) | Mission load competes with mentoring time (`PEO-004`) |
| **E** | Learner has no meaningful Mentor contact for six weeks | **UNSAFE / NOT CREDIBLE** for that learner’s mentoring claim | Continuity broken; trigger review / cover |
| **F** | Mentor uses monthly group circles but never meets learners individually | **UNSAFE / NOT CREDIBLE** | Group must not erase individual knowledge / private access |
| **G** | Highly independent Produce learner needs little routine support | **SUSTAINABLE** | Independence can reduce routine intensity; keep access open |
| **H** | Produce learner preparing major post-school transition | **SUSTAINABLE WITH CONDITIONS** | Older ≠ low need; transition intensity rises |
| **I** | Mentor has several wellbeing / safeguarding concerns simultaneously | **OVERLOADED** / escalate | Refer; do not absorb clinical/safeguarding investigation load |
| **J** | Small rural school has only two suitable Mentors | **SUSTAINABLE WITH CONDITIONS** if cohort sized / staggered; **NOT CREDIBLE** if faking large caseloads | Resource-poor strategies allowed; fake mentoring not |
| **K** | Digital dashboard says learner is “low risk,” but Mentor notices concern | Follow Mentor judgment → referral/escalation as needed | AI must not override human notice |
| **L** | Family strongly pushes one pathway against learner wishes | **Referral / facilitated dialogue**; Mentor holds learner voice | Family influence ≠ pathway control |
| **M** | Mentor absent during Choose transition period | **SUSTAINABLE WITH CONDITIONS** only with prepared cover + handover; else **NOT CREDIBLE** | Absence resilience required at peak transition |
| **N** | Learner has Mentor plus specialist Practitioner and pathway advisor | **SUSTAINABLE** if primary Mentor ownership clear | Multi-adult support OK; avoid ownership vacuum |
| **O** | Mentor is excellent relationally but does not understand Six Worlds / ADR-0018 | **Referral / development required** | Competence gap for Tarbiyat pathway mentoring (`PEO-003`) |

## 31. Core vs country vs implementation

| Layer | Includes |
|---|---|
| **Core** | Mentoring must be relational; capacity = relationship depth + available time; workload-sensitive caseload; transition intensity; primary Mentor continuity; individual visibility; referral / safeguarding boundaries; no AI replacement; overload triggers rebalance; caseload states; no universal fixed ratio |
| **Country** | Statutory pastoral ratios; counselling requirements; safeguarding laws; student welfare regulations (`GAP-037`) |
| **Implementation** | Numerical planning ranges (after pilot evidence); meeting cadence; timetable allocation; FTE; software dashboards; local Mentor job descriptions |

## 35. Explicit non-claims

This standard does **not**:

- approve ADR-0015 / ADR-0018 or close GAP-034;
- invent a universal Mentor∶learner Core ratio;
- invent counselling / therapy licence requirements;
- invent country pastoral statutory ratios (`GAP-037`);
- turn Mentors into safeguarding investigators or clinicians;
- allow AI to assign pathways or determine capacity;
- treat caseload states as staff performance rankings;
- design mentoring software or database schemas;
- require daily mentoring logs;
- claim that group circles alone equal mentoring.
