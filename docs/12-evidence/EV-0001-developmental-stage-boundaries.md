> **Purpose of this file:** Lodge cited developmental and comparative-education evidence relevant to ADR-0005’s approximate stage windows. This pack does **not** approve ADR-0005, alter its age windows, or map Malaysian Years.

---

## Purpose

Determine whether credible evidence supports, challenges, narrows, widens, or reframes ADR-0005’s developmental architecture:

`Explore → Discover → Choose → Deepen → Produce`

with overlapping windows, readiness (not birthday cut-offs), provisional concentration in Choose, and formal Major + Minor from Deepen.

## Architectural question

At approximately what developmental periods should Explore, Discover, Choose, Deepen and Produce dominate, and when should formal Major + Minor pathway selection become appropriate?

## Current hypothesis from ADR-0005

| Stage | Typical window (ADR-0005) | Pathway status |
|---|---|---|
| Explore | ~5–11 | No formal pathway |
| Discover | ~10–13 | Six Worlds exposure; no Major/Minor |
| Choose | ~12–15 | Provisional concentration only |
| Deepen | ~14–17 | Formal Major + Minor may begin |
| Produce | ~16–18+ | Advanced production within Major/Minor |

Commitment ladder: **Interest → Preference → Provisional Concentration → Major/Minor → Advanced Production**.

---

## Evidence summary

| Theme | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Broad, low-commitment early schooling (Explore) | **Strongly supports** |
| Multi-domain exposure without formal specialisation in late childhood / early adolescence (Discover) | **Reasonably supports** |
| Reversible preference / concentration in early–middle adolescence (Choose) | **Reasonably supports** |
| Formal but permeable specialisation in middle–later adolescence (Deepen) | **Reasonably supports** (comparative + career-development aligned; exact edges approximate) |
| Advanced authentic / capstone production in later adolescence (Produce) | **Reasonably supports** intensity; **insufficient** to force Produce as a hard-separate stage vs late-Deepen overlay |
| Prefer later flexible specialisation over early irreversible tracking | **Strongly supports** on equity grounds; efficiency benefits of early tracking **not** clearly demonstrated |
| Age windows + readiness (not age alone) | **Strongly supports** as architectural method |
| Precise birthday boundaries | **Evidence insufficient** — broad ranges are the responsible form |

## Explore evidence (~5–11)

### Findings that matter architecturally

1. **Foundational literacy, numeracy and social-emotional skills** are treated by UNESCO as essential building blocks from early childhood through primary schooling, not as optional enrichment. Primary years are framed as a window for foundations with lasting effects on later learning.  
   *Source:* UNESCO, *Foundational learning* overview (accessed 2026-09-14).

2. **Play / playful learning** is presented by UNICEF (with the LEGO Foundation) as central to quality early childhood pedagogy and relevant into early primary years; rote-only approaches are criticised as neglecting developmental domains (motor, cognitive, social-emotional).  
   *Source:* UNICEF & LEGO Foundation (2018), *Learning through Play*.

3. UNESCO/UNICEF primary-school health and wellbeing work targets roughly ages **5–12**, reinforcing that this span is treated as formative for broad human development, not occupational allocation.  
   *Source:* UNESCO/UNICEF (2024), *Building strong foundations* initiative materials.

4. **Early tracking into differentiated programmes around ages 10–12** is associated in international evidence with increased inequality and weaker equality of opportunity, without a clear mean-performance benefit. That is an argument against treating Explore/early Discover as formal pathway years.  
   *Sources:* Hanushek & Woessmann (2006); OECD PISA analyses of age at first selection.

### Strength rating

**Evidence strongly supports** Explore as breadth-dominant, play/inquiry-informed, low pathway commitment through roughly the primary years.

### Precision note

Evidence does **not** fix Explore’s upper edge at exactly age 11. ADR-0005’s soft transition into Discover ~10–12 is consistent with adolescence onset literature (below).

## Discover evidence (~10–13)

### Findings that matter architecturally

1. The U.S. National Academies define adolescence as beginning with puberty (~**10–12**) and emphasise that adolescents must **explore** environments to build cognitive, social and emotional skills and identity capacity. Premature foreclosure of exploration conflicts with that developmental task.  
   *Source:* National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (2019), *The Promise of Adolescence* (NASEM). NASEM subdivides: early adolescence ~10–12; middle ~13–15; late ~16–18.

2. Longitudinal vocational-interest research shows **moderate stability** and still-substantial change from late childhood into early adolescence (~11–14), with trait-like components increasing over time. Mean interest levels often **dip** in early adolescence before rising later. This argues for structured exposure and reflection, not formal occupational pathway naming.  
   *Sources:* Hoff et al. (2018) meta-analysis; Stollberg-Winkler dissertation / related early-adolescence interest studies (ages 11–14).

3. Comparative systems that keep comprehensive lower-secondary programmes until ~15–16 treat this period as broad preparation rather than hard vocational allocation (Finland/Norway-type patterns in OECD reviews). Systems that select at ~10–12 exist, but OECD and economics-of-education literature associate earlier selection with equity risks.  
   *Sources:* OECD (2023–2024) upper-secondary pathway papers; Hanushek & Woessmann (2006).

### Strength rating

**Evidence reasonably supports** Discover as structured multi-domain exposure without formal Major/Minor.

### Challenge / tension

Some high-performing early-tracking systems (e.g. parts of Germanophone Europe) begin differentiation near ages 10–12. Average performance can remain competitive; the stronger consistent finding is **equity / late-bloomer / family-background** risk, not universal cognitive harm. ADR-0005’s refusal of formal Major/Minor in Discover remains defensible for Tarbiyat’s stated equity and anti-labelling principles, even though early-tracking systems exist.

## Choose evidence (~12–15)

### Findings that matter architecturally

1. **Metacognition and decision-related self-knowledge** increase from early to middle adolescence. Middle adolescents (~15) show greater metacognitive knowledge of decision making than early adolescents (~13). Emerging metacognition also supports more independent evaluation of advice.  
   *Sources:* Mann et al. (1991); Mok et al. / related metacognition–adolescence studies (e.g. *Developmental Science* line of work on ignoring false advice).

2. NASEM’s **middle adolescence (~13–15)** is characterised by intensifying identity work, agency and purposeful self-direction — consistent with provisional educational concentrations that remain revisable.

3. Career-development theory (Super) places **exploration** and progressive clarification of vocational self-concept across adolescence, with **crystallisation** commonly associated with mid-to-late teens rather than early teens. Super also emphasised flexible recycling through stages (not rigid age locks).  
   *Sources:* Super life-span, life-space theory summaries in career-education literature (e.g. Kosine & Lewis, 2008 overview).

4. Interest profiles become more stable into middle adolescence, but crystallisation and congruence with later choices continue to evolve through late secondary years (Tracey et al. grades 8–12 findings: crystallisation increases; congruence patterns are not simply linear).

### Strength rating

**Evidence reasonably supports** Choose as a period for meaningful but reversible preferences and provisional concentration.

### Precision note

Evidence does **not** prove that age 12 is the universal start of “choice competence.” Combining window + readiness (ADR-0005) is the safer architectural form.

## Deepen evidence (~14–17)

### Findings that matter architecturally

1. Across OECD countries, **age of first selection** into differentiated upper-secondary-style programmes averages about **15**, and selection most frequently occurs at the beginning of upper secondary education. Earlier selection (~10–12) is the minority pattern and is repeatedly linked to lower equity.  
   *Sources:* OECD (2024) *Managing choice, coherence and specialisation in upper secondary education*; OECD PISA 2018/2022 stratification chapters; OECD (2023) pathway-transition papers.

2. Super’s crystallisation tasks are classically associated with roughly ages **14–18** (theory ages vary by presentation; treat as approximate developmental framing, not statute).

3. OECD emphasises **permeability** — ability to move between programmes as interests develop — especially when selection stakes are high. This supports ADR-0005’s “formal but reasonably reversible” Major/Minor with bridging (`GAP-020`), not lock-in.

4. Formal specialisation in mid-adolescence is therefore **comparatively normal** among education systems; formal specialisation in primary / early Discover is the contested early-tracking pattern.

### Strength rating

**Evidence reasonably supports** Deepen (~14–17) as a defensible window for formal Major + Minor-style specialisation **if** reversibility/permeability and Exploration Floor remain.

### Challenge / tension

- Some systems specialise later or keep comprehensive upper-secondary structures with subject options rather than named Majors.  
- Some systems specialise earlier.  
- Neither proves Tarbiyat must copy them; both show that **~14–17 is within the internationally common band** for consequential pathway differentiation, while ~10–12 formal tracking is the equity-risk band.

## Produce evidence (~16–18+)

### Findings that matter architecturally

1. Advanced secondary programmes commonly concentrate authentic research, extended projects and pre-university depth in the **final secondary years**. Example: IB Diploma Programme is designed for ages **16–19**, with core independent research (Extended Essay) and breadth constraints (HL/SL structure) — a comparative analogue to depth-plus-breadth, not a mandate to adopt IB.  
   *Source:* International Baccalaureate Organisation, Diploma Programme pages (accessed 2026-09-14).

2. IB Middle Years Programme spans **11–16**, then hands off to 16–19 programmes — a rough analogue to broad middle years then intensified senior production/specialisation (comparative observation only).

3. Qualitative and case-study research on **senior capstones / project-based learning** in upper secondary reports perceived gains in agency, research skill, self-efficacy and transfer — but evidence quality is often perception/case-based rather than large causal trials. Authenticity and mentoring appear as design conditions.  
   *Sources:* senior-capstone / PBL theses and case studies (e.g. high-school senior project transfer studies; upper-secondary–university PBL collaboration studies).

4. NASEM late adolescence (~16–18) emphasises increasing capacity for self-direction and identity consolidation — compatible with advanced production under supervision and safeguarding.

### Strength rating

**Evidence reasonably supports** later-adolescence advanced production, capstones and authentic external work.

**Evidence is insufficient** to prove Produce must be a hard-separate developmental stage rather than a high-intensity overlay on late Deepen. Comparative systems often treat senior years as a programme phase with production features inside specialisation, not as a fifth biological stage.

## Early tracking / specialisation evidence

### Fair reading of benefits claimed

Advocates argue early tracking can:

- create more homogeneous instructional groups;
- match curriculum to current performance;
- supply differentiated academic/vocational routes for labour markets.

### Fair reading of risks evidenced

Large-scale comparative work finds:

- earlier selection associated with **greater inequality** and stronger family-background effects;
- **no clear equity–efficiency trade-off** favouring early tracking (mean performance not reliably improved; sometimes tends lower);
- late bloomers and socially disadvantaged students at particular risk of misallocation when selection is early and hard to reverse;
- vocational specialisation can aid early employment but may trade off later adaptability (life-cycle findings on vocational vs general education).

*Sources:* Hanushek & Woessmann (2006); OECD PISA stratification chapters; Contini & Cugnata (2020) and related DiD tracking studies; Hanushek, Woessmann & Zhang (2011/2017 line) on vocational education life-cycle trade-offs; OECD pathway-permeability guidance.

### Implications for Tarbiyat

| Practice | Evidence posture |
|---|---|
| Formal Major/Minor in Explore / early Discover | **Challenged** (equity / labelling) |
| Provisional concentration in Choose | **Supported** as lower-stakes differentiation |
| Formal Major/Minor in Deepen with permeability | **Supported** relative to early irreversible tracking |
| Zero mobility after Deepen | **Challenged** by OECD permeability findings |

## Age versus readiness

### Findings that matter architecturally

1. Developmental science treats adolescence as a **bio-developmental span with individual variation** (NASEM), not a set of birthday switches.
2. Career-development theory explicitly allows **recycling** through stages (Super), undermining chronological lock-step.
3. Metacognition, interest stability and identity commitment develop **unevenly** across individuals and domains.
4. OECD guidance on transitions emphasises guidance, information quality and flexibility — process factors, not age alone.

### Verdict

**Evidence strongly supports** ADR-0005’s dual rule: approximate developmental windows **plus** readiness evidence under human governance.

**Evidence strongly challenges** exact-age mandatory placement.

This pack does **not** design a readiness scoring algorithm (out of scope).

## Comparative education-system observations

Critical distinction: **what systems do ≠ what developmental science requires.**

| System / pattern | Observation | Use for ADR-0005 |
|---|---|---|
| OECD modal first selection ~15 | Most common formal pathway differentiation near mid-adolescence | Supports Deepen entry; not a statute |
| Early-tracking systems (~10–12) | Exist; equity concerns dominate evidence | Challenges formal Major/Minor before Deepen |
| Finland / late comprehensive patterns | Broad lower secondary; upper-secondary choice later | Supports delayed formal specialisation |
| Singapore Full Subject-Based Banding reforms | Move toward more flexible subject-level customisation rather than rigid early streams | Comparative support for flexible concentration over early lock-in (policy direction, not Core copy) |
| IB PYP / MYP / DP continuum | Broad inquiry → middle years → 16–19 depth with HL/SL breadth | Comparative analogue for progressive depth + retained breadth |
| England-style subject narrowing (GCSEs → A-levels) | Progressive subject choice through mid/late teens | Supports staged concentration; not identical to Major/Minor |

No Malaysia mapping performed.

## Major + Minor implications

There is **no** substantial literature using Tarbiyat’s exact “Major + Minor” school terminology. Evaluate the **underlying concept**:

> progressive, reversible deepening after broad exploration, with complementary second focus and non-zero breadth

### Tests

| Proposition | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Interest → Preference → Provisional Concentration → Formal specialisation → Advanced production | **Reasonably supported** by career-development stage logic + interest-stability timelines |
| Provisional concentration in Choose (~12–15) | **Reasonably supported** |
| Formal Major/Minor beginning in Deepen (~14–17) | **Reasonably supported** vs earlier formal tracking; aligned with OECD modal selection ages |
| Formal Major/Minor in Discover or Explore | **Not supported** for Tarbiyat’s equity/anti-labelling goals; challenged by early-tracking evidence |
| Retaining Exploration Floor during specialisation | **Reasonably supported** by IB-style breadth constraints and OECD concerns about over-narrowing / dead-end tracks |

---

## Evidence tensions / contradictory findings

1. **Early-tracking systems persist** and are not uniformly low-performing; equity is the clearer casualty than mean scores.
2. **Vocational early advantage** in school-to-work transitions can be real, while long-run adaptability costs appear in some life-cycle analyses — mixed depending on outcome horizon.
3. **Interest crystallisation continues** into late adolescence and beyond; treating Deepen Major/Minor as “final career” would overclaim.
4. **Produce as distinct stage** is under-specified by developmental science; many systems embed production in senior specialised programmes.
5. **Exact window edges** (±1–2 years) are not scientifically unique; overlaps in ADR-0005 are a feature, not a bug.
6. Most PBL/capstone evidence is **not** randomised system-level causal proof.

---

## Recommended interpretation

1. Keep ADR-0005’s **overlapping approximate windows**; do not convert them into birthday laws.
2. Keep **provisional concentration in Choose** and **formal Major/Minor from Deepen**.
3. Keep **readiness + human governance** co-equal with typical age windows.
4. Treat Produce as **architecturally useful** for advanced authenticity/production intensity; allow human reviewers to decide whether it remains a named stage or may operate as late-Deepen overlay in some country structures — evidence does not force either.
5. Do **not** shift formal Major/Minor earlier to match early-tracking countries.
6. Do **not** delay all formal specialisation to the final year only; mid-adolescence formalisation with permeability is the better-supported middle path.

## Research limitations

1. Few studies evaluate a five-named-stage Lab-First school identical to Tarbiyat.
2. Comparative education ≠ developmental prescription.
3. Career-theory ages are heuristic, culturally bounded and historically dated in places.
4. Capstone/PBL evidence is often qualitative.
5. This pack did not exhaust every national academy curriculum framework; priority was OECD, UNESCO/UNICEF, NASEM, peer-reviewed tracking/interest literature and IB primary documentation.
6. No Malaysia regulatory research was undertaken (correctly out of scope).

## Explicit non-claims

This evidence pack does **not**:

- approve ADR-0004 or ADR-0005;
- change ADR-0005 age windows;
- claim OECD averages are biologically mandatory ages;
- claim IB or any national system must be copied;
- map Malaysian Years or invent Malaysian regulation;
- define timetable percentages, Competency Graph schemas, or Six Worlds competency maps;
- create a readiness scoring algorithm.
