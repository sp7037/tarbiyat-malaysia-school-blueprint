## 1. Purpose

Define how Tarbiyat schools **govern external Mission partners** so operators can determine:

- who may participate as a Mission partner;
- what authority they may exercise;
- what benefits they may receive;
- what boundaries they must respect;
- when the relationship must be restricted, suspended or terminated.

Learners gain authentic capability without:

- partner-controlled curriculum;
- learner exploitation or disguised child labour;
- advertising disguised as education;
- automatic IP capture;
- unsafe or unsupervised access to minors;
- inappropriate data access;
- commercial pressure overriding educational value;
- exclusive recruitment pipelines;
- political or ideological capture;
- unreviewed deployment.

This document resolves **GAP-022** as Core Mission Partner Governance operating architecture. It is **not** a legal contract pack, Institution-in-Residence operating manual (`MIS-002` APPROVED / GAP-023 Closed), Mission risk matrix (`MIS-003` — already APPROVED), or country partnership statute.

**Public rule of thumb:** Partners may contribute expertise, authentic context, facilities and critique. They may **not** purchase curriculum, assessment, learner access, data, IP or recruitment control.

---

## 2. Education-First governing principle

**Education-First:** external partners participate because they improve learner development, authenticity and capability (ADR-0007).

Learners do **not** exist to supply:

- cheap labour or unpaid outsourced workforce;
- sales promoters or product testers without educational justification;
- recruitment inventory;
- marketing content;
- commercial production capacity;
- political messaging;
- uncompensated IP;
- personal data;
- publicity assets.

A partner may receive legitimate value from collaboration (feedback, prototypes, community benefit, research insight). **Educational value and learner protection remain governing priorities.**

Aligns with ADR-0007 Education-First / no student exploitation and Campus Live Workflow guardrails.

## 3. What is — and is not — a Mission Partner

### 3.1 Mission Partner (governing definition)

A **Mission Partner** is an external organisation or identifiable professional engaged under an approved **Mission Partner Brief** (or equivalent proportional record) to support one or more Missions through authentic problem/context, specialist knowledge, professional methods, critique, facility access, lawful data/environment, or mentoring **within authorised scope**.

External prestige, brand recognition, funding size or advisory title alone does **not** create partner status.

### 3.2 Distinguish functions (do not blur)

| Function | Typical meaning | Mission Partner? |
|---|---|---|
| **Guest / speaker** | Occasional talk or demonstration | No — lighter presence; not full partner lifecycle |
| **Visitor** | Campus visit under visitor controls | No — access control only |
| **Vendor / supplier** | Sells goods/services to the school | No — procurement relationship |
| **Donor** | Gives money/resources without Mission operating role | No — philanthropy ≠ partner authority |
| **Sponsor** | Funds equipment, facilities, scholarships or programmes | **Not automatically** a Mission Partner; funding ≠ educational control (§16) |
| **Adviser** | High-level advice without learner operating role | No — unless separately briefed into a Mission role |
| **Practitioner** | Authorised professional educational function (ADR-0015 / PEO) | Distinct — partner may *supply* Practitioners; individual authority still governed by People architecture |
| **Practitioner-in-Residence** | Sustained individual practitioner presence | Distinct people/authorisation track — not automatic partner control |
| **Institution-in-Residence** | Sustained organisational embedding (`MIS-002`) | Higher-depth relationship that **inherits** this standard; detailed lifecycle remains `MIS-002` |

One organisation may hold more than one function (e.g. sponsor + Mission Partner). **Functions and authority must not be blurred.** Sponsorship paperwork does not substitute for partner governance; partner status does not substitute for Practitioner authorisation.

### 3.3 Partners are optional

A partner is **not** required for every authentic Mission. Internal authentic problems, simulation with authentic constraints, community problems and Campus Live Workflows may be valid without external partners (ADR-0007 authenticity A–F).

## 4. Partner role taxonomy

Roles describe **involvement in a Mission or programme**, not permanent organisational status. One organisation may hold different roles in different Missions.

| Role | Typical involvement | Not equivalent to |
|---|---|---|
| **Guest / Speaker** | Occasional talk, demonstration or inspiration; minimal ongoing operating role | Institution-in-Residence |
| **Visiting Expert** | Scoped critique, masterclass or technical input on defined topics | Mission Partner for full lifecycle |
| **Mission Contributor** | Supplies context, data (within classification), materials or one-off review without co-owning the Mission | Sustained pipeline partner |
| **Mission Partner** | Supports one or more Missions under an approved Mission Partner Brief; problem framing, expertise, structured feedback | Curriculum author or assessor |
| **Institution / Industry-in-Residence** | Sustained, governed presence or recurring professional involvement (`MIS-002`) | Guest visits twice per year; sponsorship alone |
| **Deployment / Implementation Partner** | Hosts or receives outputs for supervised real-world use or deployment (ADR-0007 Deployment Boundary) | Automatic commercial deployer |
| **Research / Innovation Partner** | University, lab or R&D organisation co-designing inquiry, publication or innovation pathways under research integrity rules | Owner of all Mission-created IP |

**Escalation of role does not escalate partner control** over education, assessment, safeguarding, pathways or IP (ADR-0007).

Map to ADR-0007: Guest speaker → Visiting expert → Mission partner → Institution-in-Residence. This standard adds **Mission Contributor**, **Deployment / Implementation Partner**, and **Research / Innovation Partner** for operational clarity.

---

## 5. Partner value test

Partner participation must add **legitimate educational value**, such as:

- authentic problem / context;
- specialist knowledge;
- professional methods;
- critique;
- facility access;
- real audience / user perspective;
- mentoring within authorised scope;
- data / environment where lawful and safe.

**Reject or refuse “partner” framing** where the main value is:

- logos / logo-wall association;
- publicity;
- recruitment access;
- sales;
- sponsorship visibility alone;
- prestige association without substantive educational contribution.

Partnership quality depends on **educational contribution and governance**, not logos, money or partner count.

## 6. Eligibility and proportional due diligence

Before a **Mission Partner** (or higher-involvement role) engages learners, the school applies **minimum eligibility principles**. Depth of diligence scales with relationship depth and risk (§7) — avoid impossible bureaucracy for a low-risk guest contribution.

| Area | Principle |
|---|---|
| Legal identity | Lawful organisation or identifiable professional; scope transparent |
| Representative authority | Person speaking for the partner has actual authority for the engagement |
| Mission relevance | Contribution fits educational purpose and Six Worlds capability development |
| Reputation / relevant history | Material adverse history considered as known and lawfully checkable |
| Safeguarding suitability | Pattern incompatible with safe work with minors → refuse or elevate |
| Conflicts of interest | Material conflicts disclosed and managed (§20) |
| Professional competence | Claims match actual capability for the scoped role |
| Financial / commercial conflicts | Funding, sales or procurement interests declared |
| Integrity | No deceptive, discriminatory or exploitative conditions on learners |
| Safety competence | Hazardous domains: partner instructions must not require unsafe learner practice |
| Facility suitability | External sites assessed under MIS-003 / ADR-0020 — partner cannot self-certify safety |
| Data / privacy implications | Purpose-limited sharing only (§14) |
| Creator Rights / IP expectations | Declared before substantial learner work (§13) |
| Learner-contact model | Channels, supervision and intensity defined (§10–§11) |
| Continuity risk | Dependency and fallback considered for sustained roles (§22) |
| Minors | Partner accepts supervised access and child-appropriate communication |
| Hidden agenda | No undisclosed primary goal of recruitment, product sales, political campaigning or IP harvesting |

**Country/project adds:** registrations, background checks, insurance, licences, statutory checks — not invented in Core.

## 7. Relationship depth and governance burden

Governance burden must match relationship depth. Categories are **qualitative and operational** — **not** numeric scores.

| Depth (illustrative) | Examples | Typical burden |
|---|---|---|
| **Light presence** | One-off guest talk; short classroom expert visit | Visitor controls; light brief; no full institutional partnership pack |
| **Scoped contribution** | Visiting Expert / Mission Contributor; limited critique or materials | Proportional brief; clear access limits; basic risk screen |
| **Mission Partner** | Recurring or multi-Mission support under brief | Unified Partner Pack (`MIS-FRM-001`); IP/data screens; conflict check; exit terms |
| **Elevated access / risk** | Off-site activity; learner data access; high-risk facility; commercially sensitive Mission | Expanded MIS-003 controls; conditional annexes; stronger supervision |
| **Institution-in-Residence** | Sustained embedding | Full MIS-001 + `MIS-002` designation path |

A short classroom expert visit must **not** require the same burden as Institution-in-Residence, off-site high-risk work, or commercially sensitive recurring partnership.

## 8. Mission intake and educationalisation

A partner’s **raw** industry, government or community problem is **not** automatically a student Mission. The partner cannot simply deliver a commercial task and declare it a Mission (ADR-0007 Mission Educationalisation).

### 8.1 Conceptual flow

```text
Partner need / problem
        ↓
Mission intake (register source, role, sponsor-if-any)
        ↓
Educational review (Educator + Practitioner [+ Mentor readiness context])
        ↓
Safeguarding / risk screen (MIS-003 APPROVED)
        ↓
Scope / age suitability
        ↓
World / capability relevance (Six Worlds)
        ↓
IP / data classification
        ↓
Approved Mission Partner Brief (proportional)
        ↓
Learner participation (voluntary where applicable)
```

### 8.2 School authority retained

Tarbiyat retains authority over:

- learning purpose;
- stage suitability;
- learner agency;
- workload;
- risk;
- evidence expectations;
- timetable integration;
- whether to simplify, reshape, anonymise, narrow, simulate components, remove commercial urgency, reject unsafe elements, or reject the entire Mission.

**Partner urgency does not override educational pacing** or Formal Recognition obligations (ADR-0003).

### 8.3 Intake outcomes

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Proceed** | Brief approved; Mission Team assigned |
| **Proceed with conditions** | e.g. authenticity lowered, data anonymised, deployment excluded |
| **Defer** | Pending partner clarification, IP/data terms, or staffing |
| **Reject** | Incompatible with Education-First, safeguarding, or capability fit |

## 9. Mission Partner Brief

A Mission Partner (or significant external contribution) receives an explicit **Partner Brief** or equivalent proportional record **before substantive participation**.

Operational record for significant external Missions: `MIS-FRM-001` (**APPROVED** — educational record, not a legal contract).

| Element | Required for significant external mission |
|---|---|
| Problem / need (educationalised) | Yes |
| Educational purpose | Yes |
| Intended learner stage / readiness | Yes |
| Mission scope and boundaries | Yes |
| Expected outputs (evidence types) | Yes |
| Authenticity level (A–F guidance) | Yes |
| Deployment expectation, if any | Yes (or explicit “none”) |
| Partner role (taxonomy §4) | Yes |
| What partner may / may not do | Yes |
| Practitioner / Educator / Mission Lead responsibilities | Yes |
| Learner role and voluntariness | Yes |
| Which learners; where; supervision | Yes |
| Data the partner may see | Yes |
| Outputs the partner may access | Yes |
| Direct communication rules | Yes |
| Off-site activity allowed? | Yes |
| IP / Background IP expectations | Yes |
| Confidentiality / publication / branding | Yes |
| Safety constraints | Yes |
| Time / workload constraints | Yes |
| Evidence / review role (partner vs school) | Yes |
| Education-First / assessment / PoC boundary | Yes |
| Escalation and stop-work / school authority | Yes |
| Exit / suspension conditions | Yes |

Ordinary internal Missions and light guest contributions may use a lighter brief consistent with the same principles. **Do not require a giant contract for every small interaction.**

## 10. Authority boundaries

Partners do **not** automatically control:

- curriculum;
- Mission educational objectives;
- assessment;
- PoC verification;
- learner pathways;
- safeguarding;
- discipline;
- school timetable;
- publication;
- learner data;
- Creator Rights / IP;
- stop-work decisions.

**Final educational and safeguarding authority rests with Tarbiyat**, not partners (ADR-0007 / ADR-0015 / MIS-003).

---

## 11. Access and role scoping

External partners must **not** automatically receive:

- direct **unsupervised** access to minors;
- learner personal contact information;
- private messaging rights with learners;
- behavioural, safeguarding or full academic records;
- Proof of Capability profiles or Capability Claims as a bulk feed;
- pathway / Major–Minor decisions;
- family information.

### 11.1 Access rules

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Purpose limitation | Access only for defined Mission or I-in-R educational purpose |
| Supervision | Partner–learner interaction supervised per safeguarding level (ADR-0015; MIS-003; country law) |
| Proportionality | Minimum people, minimum data, minimum duration |
| Developmental fit | Authenticity and contact intensity match readiness |
| Auditability | Schools should be able to account for scheduled partner contact (implementation SOP) |
| No vague “partner access” | Brief states who, what, where, under what supervision, what data/outputs, communication rights, off-site yes/no |

Institution-in-Residence **does not** imply unrestricted campus or learner access (`MIS-002`).

---

## 12. Partner communication

Unmanaged private access to learners is prohibited as an operating principle.

| Channel | Default posture |
|---|---|
| School-authorised email / LMS / scheduled video | Preferred for Mission work |
| Phone | Only where briefed and supervised/controlled |
| Messaging apps / social media / private DMs | **Not** default; generally prohibited for partner–learner contact |
| After-hours contact | Not permitted outside approved Mission arrangements |
| One-to-one private channels | Require explicit school authorisation; default is supervised / group / school-mediated |

Famous or senior professionals receive **no exemption**. Country law on communications with minors remains Country Profile work — Core does not invent statutes.

---

## 13. Creator Rights / IP

Operationalise ADR-0014 **before significant learner work begins** where reasonably possible:

| Topic | Brief must clarify |
|---|---|
| Partner Background IP | What pre-exists; licence for educational use only |
| School Background IP | Tools, platforms, facilities — no surprise claims |
| Learner pre-existing work | Not absorbed by partner |
| Expected Mission-created work | Classification path (Ordinary → Significant → Protectable, etc.) |
| Confidentiality | What cannot be published |
| Publication / portfolio | Including sanitised capability evidence |
| Use rights | Licence vs ownership claims |
| Contribution recording | Contribution Record expectations |
| Commercial interest | Disclosure; Creator Rights Review triggers |

Partner participation does **not** automatically confer:

- learner IP ownership;
- exclusive licence;
- publication rights;
- commercialisation rights;
- student image rights.

**Forbidden defaults:**

- “Everything students make belongs to the sponsor.”
- “Everything touched by a learner is learner-owned regardless of joint development or Background IP.”

Unexpected valuable work triggers Creator Rights Review — **not** silent capture. Templates: `MIS-FRM-005` (**APPROVED**; country IP mapping `GAP-026` remains open).

## 14. Data / privacy

Align with ADR-0007 data classes; operational labels for partner Missions:

| Class | Partner access |
|---|---|
| **Open / public** | May be shared when appropriate |
| **School-internal (educational)** | Shared only under Mission governance |
| **Partner-confidential** | Minimum necessary; learner sees only approved subset |
| **Learner-sensitive** | Not shared with partner without lawful basis and safeguards |
| **Restricted / safety-sensitive** | Partner must not expose learners (e.g. raw identifiable health records, classified material) |

A Mission Partner does **NOT** automatically receive:

- full student profile;
- family information;
- safeguarding information;
- counselling / health information;
- unrelated PoC records;
- school-wide datasets.

Only the **minimum appropriate** information may be shared under applicable law and school governance. Country privacy law: Country Profile work (`GAP-006` / `GAP-011` where relevant).

## 15. PoC / assessment boundary

Partners may contribute **authentic critique**. Partner status does **not** automatically confer:

- sole assessment authority;
- universal verification authority;
- ability to award Tarbiyat capability claims;
- power to demand favourable results.

| Partner may provide | School / human governance retains |
|---|---|
| Technical feedback | Interpretation for Formal Recognition (ADR-0003) |
| Standards-based critique | Final capability judgment (ADR-0016; ADR-0015 multi-stream) |
| User / stakeholder usefulness feedback | Whether usefulness equals competence |
| Observations for evidence record | Verification and Capability Claims |

**Stakeholder praise ≠ capability certification** (ADR-0016). Conflict of interest must be considered when the same partner funds, recruits, sells and verifies (§20). Detailed PoC ops remain `POC-001` DRAFT.

---

## 16. Fees, sponsorship and funding

Partner funding may support Mission materials, equipment, practitioner time, research, facilities, scholarships or lawful programme support.

Funding must **not** automatically grant:

- curriculum influence;
- assessment influence;
- recruitment preference;
- exclusive learner access;
- learner-data access;
- IP ownership;
- branding rights beyond agreement;
- protection from stop-work / restriction / suspension / termination.

**Funding and educational authority remain distinct.** Core sets **no** fee percentages or sponsorship tiers.

---

## 17. Commercial boundary

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Generally acceptable** | Real specifications (educationalised); authentic quality standards; prototype feedback; supervised testing; stakeholder review; real-world problem context |
| **Higher scrutiny** | Production for sale; customer delivery; revenue-linked outputs; repeated operational work; partner deadline dependence; commercial deployment |
| **Reject** | Production quotas; replacing paid workers with learners; repetitive low-learning tasks; learner output mainly for partner profit; sales targets on learners |

Connect to ADR-0007 Campus Live Workflow guardrails and Scenario F (repetitive commercial assembly).

---

## 18. Branding / publicity

Partners cannot automatically use for marketing or publicity:

- Tarbiyat name;
- student names;
- student images;
- learner work;
- Mission results;
- PoC claims.

Any permitted use needs appropriate agreement, consent, privacy and Creator Rights treatment. Enrolment or Mission participation is **not** promotional consent (ADR-0014).

---

## 19. Recruitment and marketing boundary

Partners must **not** use school access primarily to:

- recruit minors;
- build exclusive talent pipelines;
- collect marketing leads;
- promote products or services to learners/families;
- extract endorsements or testimonials without consent governance;
- impose compulsory internship or employment pressure;
- steer pathways for partner workforce needs.

**Permitted:** career exposure, standards insight, authentic professional context — proportionate and Education-First.

Legitimate later-stage opportunities (e.g. Produce-stage learner interest in employment) may exist under **separate**, learner-protective school/country policy — **not** as hidden Mission terms or purchased access.

---

## 20. Conflicts of interest

Material conflicts require **disclosure** and **human review** (Creator Rights Review or partner governance review as appropriate):

- staff or family financial interest in partner or venture;
- partner investment in learner work;
- staff-owned companies sponsoring Missions;
- parent-owned company Missions;
- procurement or sponsorship tied to curriculum favours;
- licensing revenue from learner output;
- assessor / verifier who is also partner representative;
- partner that funds, recruits, sells equipment, supplies facility **and** seeks verification or publicity in one relationship;
- mentor/practitioner equity in learner Micro-Venture (ADR-0014).

**No single relationship should silently combine incompatible authority.** Educational advisers must **not** secretly negotiate against learner interests. Detailed procurement law: country implementation.

---

## 21. External facilities

External facilities may expand Tarbiyat capability (ADR-0020).

**External facility access ≠ externalised institutional responsibility.**

Access conditions, supervision, suitability and safety remain governed by the school under MIS-003. Partner cannot self-certify that:

- the site is safe;
- the activity is safe;
- the adult is suitable;
- equipment is appropriate.

---

## 22. Continuity and dependency

Pilot or school operation should **not** collapse if one partner withdraws (IMP-001 context).

For sustained or Mission-critical partner relationships, consider:

- dependency concentration;
- fallback authentic Mission / internal alternative;
- learner continuity;
- unfinished Mission handling;
- data return / retention;
- equipment / facility continuity;
- communication to learners.

Core sets **no** universal backup ratio. Sole-dependency on one partner with no fallback is an implementation risk (IMP-001 Gate G context) — not solved by pretending partners are mandatory.

---

## 23. Suspension, restriction, termination and exit

The school may **limit, suspend, terminate or refuse renewal** when:

- safeguarding concern or breach;
- exploitation or boundary breach (commercial, data, communication);
- pressure on assessment / PoC;
- undisclosed conflict;
- data misuse or IP misconduct;
- inappropriate learner contact;
- unsafe practice or instructions;
- persistent scope violations;
- political/commercial capture of Mission purpose;
- reputational / legal concerns where materially relevant;
- repeated failure to meet supervision or brief commitments.

**Financial contribution must not shield a partner from exit.** Stop-work does not require sponsor approval (MIS-003).

### 23.1 Learner protection during exit

If a partner relationship ends mid-Mission, protect:

- learning continuity;
- assessment fairness;
- PoC evidence;
- learner contribution records;
- confidentiality;
- emotional / relationship impacts where relevant.

**Learners must not be academically punished** because a partner relationship fails. Mission may pivot, simulate remaining work, or substitute alternative authentic experience.

## 24. People architecture boundary

Apply ADR-0015 (PEO standards remain DRAFT — not approved by this document):

- Partner representative ≠ automatically Practitioner;
- Practitioner ≠ automatically teacher / Educator;
- Professional licence ≠ automatically child-access authority;
- A partner may provide authorised Practitioners, but **individual** authority is governed through People architecture / PEO processes (`PEO-001` / `PEO-002` context; `MIS-FRM-002` / `007` / `008`).

## 25. External-adult governance and MIS-003 handoff

Partner status does **not** bypass APPROVED MIS-003:

- safeguarding preparation;
- supervision;
- visitor controls;
- communication restrictions;
- photography / image rules;
- data access limits;
- stop-work authority.

Famous or senior professionals receive **no exemption**. Partner briefs must record a **risk-screen outcome**. Partner urgency does not lower controls. Partner Missions **must not bypass** MIS-003.

Detailed R0–R4 classification, stop-work, restart, off-site and incident rules: `MISSION_RISK_AND_SAFEGUARDING.md` (`MIS-003`, APPROVED).

## 26. Relationship to Institution / Industry-in-Residence (`MIS-002`)

Partner behaviour, eligibility, access, IP/data, conflicts, funding limits and exit rules in this standard (**MIS-001**) are **prerequisites** for Institution / Industry-in-Residence recognition.

**Sustained embedding, presence/recurrence, practitioner continuity, space models, relationship review and I-in-R designation rules** remain in:

`docs/06-missions-and-production/INSTITUTION_IN_RESIDENCE.md` (`MIS-002`, APPROVED) — **GAP-023 Closed**.

Sponsored labs or funding alone are **not** I-in-R. Typical path: successful Mission Partner engagement (or equivalent onboarding review) **before** I-in-R designation.

## 27. Partner diversity and small-campus fit

Valid partners may include, where appropriate:

- businesses;
- universities;
- NGOs;
- public bodies;
- community organisations;
- laboratories;
- arts / cultural institutions;
- professional bodies;
- independent specialists.

**“Industry” is not the only authentic source.** A resource-constrained school may use a smaller, well-governed partner network — quality over count or funding size.

---

## 28. Political and public-institution sensitivity

Missions may study **real public problems** (Society / Public Systems World) with evidence and age-appropriate ethics.

Partner participation must **not** convert Tarbiyat into:

- partisan advocacy machinery;
- institutional propaganda;
- compulsory political messaging.

**Prohibited as Mission design:** partisan campaigning, electoral mobilisation, ideological indoctrination, or political promotion on behalf of a partner.

NGOs, government and advocacy bodies remain eligible as **Mission Partners** when the educationalised brief is inquiry, service design, evidence-based analysis, or civic capability — not advocacy operations.

Respect FND-003 character formation and country profile expression of civic/national identity. Do not invent political policy beyond APPROVED Core.

---

## 29. Anti-theatre test

MIS-001 prevents treating the following as genuine partnership quality:

- logo-wall partnerships;
- “industry-linked” claims based on one guest talk;
- sponsored labs presented as I-in-R;
- famous advisory names with no learner interaction;
- partner count used as quality metric;
- funding amount used as authenticity metric.

## 30. Hostile scenario tests

| ID | Scenario | Classification | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Sponsor funds lab and wants curriculum influence | **PROHIBITED** | §16 — funding ≠ curriculum control |
| **B** | Company wants student data to identify recruits | **PROHIBITED** | §14 / §19 — purpose-limited data; no recruitment mining |
| **C** | Partner wants sole PoC verification | **PROHIBITED** | §15 — school retains verification authority |
| **D** | CEO asks to message learners directly (private apps) | **RESTRICT** / **PROHIBITED** as framed | §12 — no unmanaged private access; school channels only |
| **E** | Partner provides unsafe workshop | **SUSPEND** / **TERMINATE**; stop-work | §21 / §25 — partner cannot self-certify safety; MIS-003 |
| **F** | Partner owns expensive equipment and refuses stop-work | **PROHIBITED** posture; school stop-work prevails | MIS-003 — sponsor approval not required for stop-work |
| **G** | Company provides real problem but expects production quota | **PROHIBITED** as framed; **PERMITTED WITH CONDITIONS** if educationalised without quota | §2 / §8 / §17 |
| **H** | NGO wants student photos for fundraising | **REQUIRES ESCALATION** | §18 — consent / privacy / Creator Rights; not automatic |
| **I** | Partner wants all Mission IP | **PROHIBITED** as default | §13 / ADR-0014 — may negotiate case-by-case only with Creator Rights Review |
| **J** | Partner withdraws halfway through Mission | **PERMITTED** exit; school activates continuity | §22 / §23 — learners not academically punished |
| **K** | University wants access to full learner profiles | **PROHIBITED** | §14 — minimum necessary only |
